#+TITLE: spyglass benchmarks
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil

* Method

=make bench= runs two batch Emacs processes: the driver, and an agent it starts on this host or, with =BENCH_REMOTE=, on another through SSH. Every scenario runs in both directions: =agent→driver= has the agent own the buffer and the driver mirror it, =driver→agent= the reverse. Each ends by comparing digests of the owner's buffer and of its mirror: text, mirrored properties and overlays; =exact= says whether they agree. Scenarios are described in =test/spyglass-bench-common.el=; every Emacs runs under =timeout= and kills itself past 2 GB of resident memory.

Columns:

- =MB=: size of the buffer at the end.
- =time s=: for =large-*=, the snapshot, from the subscription to the last frame acknowledged; otherwise the producer's run plus the drain until the viewer acknowledged everything. The overlays scenario's drain waits for two sweeps of the buffer, which find overlays remade far from changed text.
- =wire MB=: bytes the owner sent.
- =lat ms=: from a change on the owner to its update applied on the viewer, through the clock offset estimated by heartbeats (median/99th percentile). For changes made without hooks nor hints (the =unhinted= scenario), from their detection.
- =ack ms=: from the change to its acknowledgment back on the owner.
- =tick ms=: main-thread time of each of the owner's flushes, capture and sending (median/99th percentile/maximum); garbage collections falling in a flush count in it.
- =owner CPU s (alone)=: the owner's processor time during the scenario, and the producer's alone on a buffer no one sees.
- =GC=: garbage collections and their seconds on owner/viewer.
- =RSS MB=: peak resident memory of owner/viewer; processes run every scenario in turn, and Emacs keeps what it allocated, so later rows inherit earlier peaks.
- =exact=: digests agree; =rN= resumptions after broken streams, =bN= the N breaks; =fN= comparisons of the whole buffer the owner made; =sN= sizes the viewer's window took and the owner's terminal reached, in the =resize= scenario, whose =time s= is the total and =lat ms= the median and maximum from showing a window to the owner's terminal taking its size (driver owning) or the driver's mirror learning it back (agent owning).

Emacs 31 runs here with =gc-cons-percentage= 1.0. A batch Emacs waits for events in =spyglass-bench-step=, never longer than until its next timer (see the design notes).

Notes:

- Every row is exact. The snapshot of 100 MB takes 0.7 s; 100 000 appends of a line in three insertions (crdt.el's failing case) arrive byte for byte with a 20 ms median latency, the 16 ms of =spyglass-flush-delay= included; a 50 MB/s stream keeps up with a median of 25–28 ms.
- Ticks stay at their 4 ms budget under load (median), whatever the buffer; their 99th percentiles of 15–25 ms are garbage collections falling in them.
- Terminals: the hinted screens (=term-*=) send what changed only, about 0.2 MB/s for 60 rewrites a second; the unhinted one is found by comparing the whole buffer (=f77=, =f74=: at most a tenth of the time). Eat's own processing of 300 000 lines takes 12 s of processor time, mirroring a small part of it; vterm and Ghostel cost spyglass under 1 ms a tick. Eat's latencies vary from run to run, 1.6 to 3.6 s at the 99th percentile: Eat's own processing saturates the owner, which captures between its batches of output.
- Each broken stream resumed (=r3=, after the three breaks =b3= the scenario makes), without a new snapshot. No other scenario broke its stream.
- =resize=: a viewer's window took four sizes and the owner's terminal each of them (=s4=), within 1–3 ms of the report on the owner and 17 ms back to the viewer (the owner describes its terminal's size at its next flush).
- =*-chatter=: both ends ping each other and send a small channel message every 2 ms throughout, so that writes waiting on the stream meet frames sent by timers and filters, which the connection queues behind the frame being written (see the design notes). Every frame decoded, no stream broke, and the rows match their quiet twins. =large-10M-4MB-frames= and its chatty twin send the snapshot in frames of 4 M characters, far larger than the sockets' buffers: their ticks are longer (20 ms median), since each frame is read from the buffer, encoded and written in one go, which is why frames stay at 64 K characters by default.
- =channels=: each end sends 2 000 small messages a millisecond apart and large ones of 4 MB among them, both at once (eight from the side named first in =dir=, two back), 32 MB and 8 MB of JSON: all arrive in order, their digests equal, in 3.2–3.5 s. In this row =MB= and =wire MB= are the larger set's text and its channel bytes, =lat ms= the latency of the small messages going with it, =ack ms= that of those coming back: 0.1 ms at the median, 15–40 ms at the 99th percentile, when they wait behind a large message's chunks.

Notes:

- Every row is exact, both ways, and no stream broke but those the reconnect scenario breaks. Terminal emulators were not run on Core: Eat needs a pty, and vterm and Ghostel modules were not built there.
- From Reaver to Core, SSH carries the 50 MB/s stream whole, 35–40 ms median latency. From Core, the Windows batch Emacs is the bottleneck: its producer and spyglass together saturate its processor (3.9 s in 4 s), so the owner coalesces, sending 135 MB of the 200 MB written; memory stays bounded (86 MB) and the mirror ends exact. With =spyglass-window= at 1 MB, the Reaver-to-Core stream stalled at about 40 MB/s: hence the 4 MB default.
- Windows timers have a resolution near 16 ms: producers timed every millisecond run slower on Core (=appends=, =overlays=, =channels=), and the Windows owner's processor time includes the benchmark's own waiting loop, the =alone= figure too; compare latencies and ticks rather than processor time there.
- Latencies across hosts go through a clock offset estimated by heartbeats, good to about 10 ms (hence a negative median for the unhinted terminal, whose latency counts from detection).
- After a broken stream, SSH takes about a second to connect again: one resumption per run (=r1=), the later drops falling while it reconnects, and latencies near 1 s for the updates waiting meanwhile.
- =resize=: the Windows agent's mirror, in windows cut from its batch frame, sized the Mac's terminal, and the reverse, each size reached (=s4=): 1–3 ms from the report to the owner's terminal, 20–45 ms back to the viewer through SSH. Windows has no pty for Eat, so the scenario sizes a buffer standing for a terminal, without a process, on both hosts; real Eat, vterm and Ghostel terminals are sized in the tests (=spyglass-window-test.el=) and, parked, in a graphical Emacs (=spyglass-gui-test.el=).
- =*-chatter=: pings and channel messages every 2 ms both ways, while frames wait on SSH's pipes: every frame decoded, nothing broke.
- =large-10M-4MB-frames=: frames of 4 M characters, written from Reaver into SSH's pipe, take 1.5 s each, as many as the 64 K frames of =large-10M= take altogether: a write the pipe cannot take makes Emacs wait 20 ms before trying again, unless input arrives meanwhile (=send_process=), and a 64 KB pipe takes about 2.7 MB/s that way, as a 4 MB =process-send-string= to =myssh core 'cat > /dev/null'= measures, in writes of 64 KB or 4 MB alike. Input arriving wakes it sooner: with chatter, 0.95 s a frame. Updates stay at =spyglass-frame-chars= (64 K) by default, each write waiting once at most.
- =channels=: both sets arrive in order, digests equal, in 11–14 s, Core's timers pacing its small messages. Small messages take 0.7–1.5 ms at the median, except those Reaver sends with its eight large ones (380 ms): they wait behind the chunks, which SSH's pipe takes slowly, as above. Before frames read during a handler's write were queued behind those already read (see the design notes), messages from Core arrived out of order here: their acknowledgments' writes waited on the pipe, the filter ran again inside them, and handled the frames it read before the rest of the outer call's.

* Parking frames, in a graphical Emacs

=make test-gui= prints these, on Reaver (macOS 26), with the Mac port (Emacs 31.1) and with GNU Emacs's Cocoa build (30.2), each started with =-Q=: ten shared buffers, each watched by a mirror, receive a line before each of 500 forced redisplays, hidden (not needing a window), parked, then shown in ten windows of the visible frame; then one parked window sized by its viewer.
