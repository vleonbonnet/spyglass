#+TITLE: spyglass benchmarks
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil

* Method

=make bench= runs two batch Emacs processes: the driver, and an agent it starts on this host or, with =BENCH_REMOTE=, on another through SSH. Every scenario runs in both directions: =agent→driver= has the agent own the buffer and the driver mirror it, =driver→agent= the reverse. Each ends by comparing digests of the owner's buffer and of its mirror: text, mirrored properties and overlays; =exact= says whether they agree. Scenarios are described in =test/spyglass-bench-common.el=; every Emacs runs under =timeout= and kills itself past 2 GB of resident memory.

Columns:

- =MB=: size of the buffer at the end.
- =time s=: for =large-*=, the snapshot, from the subscription to the last frame acknowledged; otherwise the producer's run plus the drain until the viewer acknowledged everything. The overlays scenario's drain waits for two sweeps of the buffer, which find overlays remade far from changed text.
- =wire MB=: bytes the owner sent.
- =lat ms=: from a change on the owner to its update applied on the viewer, through the clock offset estimated by heartbeats (median/99th percentile). For changes made without hooks nor hints (the =unhinted= scenario), from their detection.
- =ack ms=: from the change to its acknowledgment back on the owner.
- =tick ms=: main-thread time of each of the owner's flushes, capture and sending (median/99th percentile/maximum); garbage collections falling in a flush count in it.
- =owner CPU s (alone)=: the owner's processor time during the scenario, and the producer's alone on a buffer no one sees.
- =GC=: garbage collections and their seconds on owner/viewer.
- =RSS MB=: peak resident memory of owner/viewer; processes run every scenario in turn, and Emacs keeps what it allocated, so later rows inherit earlier peaks.
- =exact=: digests agree; =rN= resumptions after N broken streams; =fN= comparisons of the whole buffer the owner made; =sN= sizes the viewer's window took and the owner's terminal reached, in the =resize= scenario, whose =time s= is the total and =lat ms= the median and maximum from showing a window to the owner's terminal taking its size (driver owning) or the driver's mirror learning it back (agent owning).

Emacs 31 runs here with =gc-cons-percentage= 1.0. A batch Emacs waits for events in =spyglass-bench-step=, never longer than until its next timer (see the design notes).

Notes:

- Every row is exact. The snapshot of 100 MB takes 0.6–0.7 s; 100 000 appends of a line in three insertions (crdt.el's failing case) arrive byte for byte with a 20 ms median latency, the 16 ms of =spyglass-flush-delay= included; a 50 MB/s stream keeps up with a median of 25–28 ms.
- Ticks stay at their 4 ms budget under load (median), whatever the buffer; their 99th percentiles of 15–25 ms are garbage collections falling in them.
- Terminals: the hinted screens (=term-*=) send what changed only, about 0.2 MB/s for 60 rewrites a second; the unhinted one is found by comparing the whole buffer (=f77=, =f90=: at most a tenth of the time). Eat's own processing of 300 000 lines takes 12 s of processor time, mirroring a small part of it; vterm and Ghostel cost spyglass under 1 ms a tick.
- Each broken stream resumed (=r3=), without a new snapshot.
- =resize=: a viewer's window took four sizes and the owner's terminal each of them (=s4=), within 1–3 ms of the report on the owner and 17 ms back to the viewer (the owner describes its terminal's size at its next flush). Owners now offer viewers to size terminals: the rows of the other scenarios match those measured before, within their noise. Eat's latencies vary from run to run, 0.1 to 3.6 s at the 99th percentile, the same before and after: Eat's own processing of 300 000 lines saturates the owner, which captures between its batches of output.

Notes:

- Every row is exact, both ways. Terminal emulators were not run on Core: Eat needs a pty, and vterm and Ghostel modules were not built there.
- From Reaver to Core, SSH carries the 50 MB/s stream whole, 33 ms median latency. From Core, the Windows batch Emacs is the bottleneck: its producer and spyglass together saturate its processor (3.8 s in 4 s), so the owner coalesces, sending 124 MB of the 200 MB written; memory stays bounded (84 MB) and the mirror ends exact. With =spyglass-window= at 1 MB, the Reaver-to-Core stream stalled at about 40 MB/s: hence the 4 MB default.
- Windows timers have a resolution near 16 ms: producers timed every millisecond run slower on Core (=appends=, =overlays=), and the Windows owner's processor time includes the benchmark's own waiting loop, the =alone= figure too; compare latencies and ticks rather than processor time there.
- Latencies across hosts go through a clock offset estimated by heartbeats, good to about 10 ms (hence a negative median for the unhinted terminal, whose latency counts from detection).
- After a broken stream, SSH takes about a second to connect again: one resumption per run (=r1=), the later drops falling while it reconnects, and latencies near 1 s for the updates waiting meanwhile.
